\begin{abstract}
We ask what FraudShield-EAC, a seeded synthetic benchmark of East African digital payments (\nChannels{}
channels, mobile money and USSD among them, in \nCountries{} countries), can and
cannot show. It is easy in a specific way: a single velocity feature separates fraud from legitimate
payments at an AUC of \nGateFloor{} on its own, so every ranking metric of the production model is
reported as a margin over it.

Trained on all \nFvTrainRows{} training rows, a calibrated XGBoost and LightGBM ensemble reaches an AUC
of \nFvAUC{}, a margin of \nFvMargin{}. Its recall at the specified flag threshold, \nFvRecallSix{},
misses the target of \nTargetRecall{}, and more training data did not close the gap in any run
measured. At a fixed \nTrainUsed{} rows, the corpus they were drawn from moved mean recall across a
range of \nSelMeanRange{}, against \nFvSeedGainFixed{} from \nTrainUsed{} rows to the whole period on one
corpus; no interval was computed for these differences. On the diagnostic model, ablations measure
redundancy rather than importance, and leave-one-country-out and a fraud variant held out in time are
both null. Refitting without each fraud type in turn costs little recall at a \nFPRBudget{}
false-positive budget for velocity, SIM-swap and mule fraud and much more for agent fraud and
synthetic identity.

The production model's one failure on a pre-specified variant is a variant without burst structure
(a single transfer, not a multi-row incident), modelled on a documented Kenyan mobile-money
scam and committed with the diagnostic model's predicted recall and its generator code before any
evidence. It is never caught at the flag threshold; at the same budget the model trained on
\nTrainUsed{} rows catches \nGateRevNewBudgetCaught{} of the \nGateRevNewBudgetRows{} rows whose
recipient the account had never paid and none of the \nGateRevKnownBudgetRows{} sent to a known payee,
small counts from an observational split that do not isolate a cause. Every fraud parameter is a
modelling choice; we state what a validation on real data would need to show.
\end{abstract}
