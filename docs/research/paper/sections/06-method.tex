\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{The production model}

XGBoost \cite{chen2016xgboost} and LightGBM \cite{ke2017lightgbm} are trained with native handling of missing values and categories, class
imbalance addressed by weighting (no oversampling), and early stopping on the validation split.
Their raw probabilities are combined as $\nWeightXGB\,p_{\mathrm{xgb}} +
\nWeightLGB\,p_{\mathrm{lgb}}$, and \textbf{one} isotonic regression \cite{zadrozny2002calibration} is fitted on the combined
score using the calibration split (resolution D-05), which is chronologically later than the
validation data and
never used for fitting or scoring otherwise. The calibrated ensemble score is the quantity the
decision thresholds and the calibration error are measured on. The specification's reason for one
calibration of the combined score (resolution D-05) is that calibrating each model separately and
averaging them does not yield a calibrated ensemble; we did not measure that alternative. In the evaluated run the
early-stopped models used \nGateXGBRounds{} (XGBoost) and \nGateLGBRounds{} (LightGBM) rounds,
with a positive-class weight of \nGateSPW{}.

An Isolation Forest \cite{liu2008iforest} is fitted on imputed features (it cannot take NaN) and its score is mapped to
a percentile of its training reference distribution, so that the anomaly score lies in $[0, 1]$
(resolution D-06). The specification routes transactions flagged only by the anomaly score to a
separate review queue that does not hold the payment; that routing belongs to the decision
engine (M6) and is not evaluated here.

\paragraph{Corpus and cache.} Features are computed over a \emph{corpus}, the most recent transactions
of the draw in time order; its \emph{corpus depth} is how many it holds, so a deeper corpus reaches
further back and gives each row more history. A \emph{cache} is the feature matrix computed from one
corpus and stored, which every later fit on that corpus reads.

\paragraph{Training volume.} The declared gate model, the one model whose run was declared in
advance to be the gate's result, was trained on \nTrainUsed{} rows holding
\nGateTrainFraud{} observed fraud, a subset of the \nTrainRows{} rows in the training period.
The backlog records the reason as the cost of the feature pass, about \nFeaturePassRate{} rows a
second on the development machine (PB-67); no document records why \nTrainUsed{} rather than another
size. The same design was later trained on every one of the \nFvTrainRows{} training rows, on a
GitHub-hosted runner (PB-66; Section~\ref{sec:volume}). Figures in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} say
which model they describe; the diagnostic battery below is at \nTrainUsed{} rows throughout.

\subsection{Explanations}

Exact TreeSHAP \cite{lundberg2017shap,lundberg2020trees} is computed per model in log-odds space, where additivity is exact, and
the analyst-facing contribution is the weighted combination $\nWeightXGB\,\phi_{\mathrm{xgb}} +
\nWeightLGB\,\phi_{\mathrm{lgb}}$ in log-odds space, annotated with its base value and the weighted sum of the models' log-odds
outputs, which is not the log-odds of the combined score (the probabilities, not the log-odds, are
averaged). We never claim that SHAP values sum to the calibrated probability: isotonic calibration is
monotone but not additive. The specification requires plain-language explanations to come from
deterministic, localised templates per feature and never from a language model. \textbf{No such
template exists.} The scorer emits a template key per feature, but no catalogue of wording has been
written (PB-132): the owner decided that wording analysts act on is a domain question, and that it is
not to be written by the engineer. The analyst console, which would present explanations, \textbf{is
not part of this paper's release and is not evaluated}: its implementation is on a separate branch that
is not merged. ADR 0084 records its design decision to show a ranked table of feature contributions in
place of templated text; nothing in this paper depends on that table, and no analyst has used it.

\subsection{The diagnostic battery}

The analyses of feature groups, slices, variants and countries (Sections~\ref{sec:evaluation}
and~\ref{sec:generalisation}) use a separate, deliberately simple model: one untuned XGBoost
booster on \nTrainUsed{} training rows, calibrated with Platt scaling (a sigmoid fitted with a slope and an offset) \cite{platt2000probabilistic}. It is a
diagnostic of the benchmark, not the production model, and its figures are labelled as such
(ADR 0029). Ablations of the production ensemble are reported with the gate.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Operating points} (resolutions D-01, D-02): recall at a \nFPRBudget{}
        false-positive rate (FPR) replaces the specification's precision at that rate, which is
        mathematically unreachable at this base rate; recall, F1 and the false-negative rate are
        read at the flag threshold \nFlagThreshold{}, the false-positive rate at the block
        threshold \nBlockThreshold{}.
  \item \textbf{Uncertainty}: \nResamples{} stratified bootstrap resamples
        \cite{efron1979bootstrap} for gate metrics, Hanley--McNeil intervals \cite{hanley1982auc}
        for AUCs of single features, Wilson intervals \cite{wilson1927interval} for
        recall on small variant counts, and the mean and standard deviation over \nSeeds{} refits.
        \textbf{All of these treat test rows as independent, and they are not}: much of the fraud is staged as
        multi-row incidents on one account, and an account's rows share its history. We therefore
        also report an account-clustered bootstrap, which resamples whole accounts (\nResamples{}
        resamples, stratified by whether an account has any fraud), for the gate model's AUC, recall at
        the budget and at the flag threshold, F1 and USSD AUC (Table~\ref{tab:clustered}), the
        diagnostic model's group AUCs, the variant recalls, the learning curve,
        leave-one-country-out and the single-feature floor. A difference between models' AUCs
        is given as a paired clustered interval, both evaluated on each resample; no $p$-value is
        reported, because the tests that produce them assume independent rows. These come from
        reproductions of the models that matched the published figures
        (Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}). The other intervals treat rows as independent and are
        narrower than they should be by an amount not measured, except where each row is on its own
        account (as for the reversal variant): those of the earlier-draw
        battery and its variant recalls, the Hanley--McNeil column of the floor's re-run, the
        row-bootstrap intervals of the full-volume model's own evidence file and of PB-66's
        monthly and precision--recall rows, and the Wilson intervals of the fairness table and
        of leave-one-fraud-type-out, and the diagnostic model's per-channel AUC intervals
        in the evidence files. The alert-budget table's precision intervals resample whole
        days, neither accounts nor rows; its recall intervals are bootstrapped, but their resampling
        unit is not recorded in the evidence file.
  \item \textbf{The floor}: every gate ranking metric is reported beside the best single feature and
        the best trivial rule (one threshold on one feature) on the same rows, as a margin
        (Section~\ref{sec:floor}).
  \item \textbf{Test discipline}: nothing is tuned on the test period; scoring runs on the test
        set are logged, with earlier runs, and one earlier read made for this paper, back-filled
        from committed evidence (Section~\ref{sec:dataset}); tests fail if the production calibrator
        learns from rows other than the calibration rows, or if the diagnostic battery's calibrator
        is fitted on test rows.
  \item \textbf{Scale}: every figure states the rows and fraud count it rests on, because
        the single-column AUC of the strongest dataset column moves with dataset size by more than
        seed noise
        (spread \nSizeSeriesSpan{} across sizes against a seed-to-seed standard deviation of
        \nSeedSdSizeSeries{}), for a reason that remains unexplained.
\end{itemize}
