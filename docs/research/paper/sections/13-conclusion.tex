\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

FraudShield-EAC was built to develop and evaluate a fraud detection system for East African digital
payments without real customer data. The benchmark sets a high floor: one velocity feature alone
separates the test rows at \nGateFloor{}. Trained on every one of the \nFvTrainRows{} rows of the
training period, the specified ensemble reaches an AUC of \nFvAUC{}, a margin of \nFvMargin{} over that
feature on the same rows. Its recall at the flag threshold, \nFvRecallSix{} \nFvRecallSixci{}, still
misses the specified target; \textbf{training volume did not close the gap in any run measured}, from
\nTrainUsed{} rows to the whole training period, while changing only the corpus \nTrainUsed{} training rows
were drawn from moved mean recall at the flag threshold across a range of \nSelMeanRange{}, which seed noise widens
by an amount not measured, against
\nFvSeedGainFixed{} from \nTrainUsed{} rows to the whole period on one fixed corpus. That holds at the
flag threshold only: of the other metrics fixed in advance, recall at the false-positive budget moved
by similar amounts under either change and AUC moved more with volume, and no interval was computed for
these differences; the metrics were fixed in advance, though not blind to the test period. Most of what a reader would like to learn
from the benchmark it cannot teach. On the diagnostic model, leave-one-group-out ablations measure
redundancy, because \nGroupsAboveCeiling{} disjoint groups each exceed the single-feature ceiling
alone, and leave-one-country-out and the novel variant held out in time are null, which we infer follows from fraud
parameters not keyed by country and from the novel variant's multi-row shape respectively; the country
holdout also shares its feature computation and target encodings with the held-out country, an effect
not measured. Refitting the full-volume model without one fraud type at a time (never fitting on it,
not never seeing it) costs little recall at the \nFPRBudget{} budget for velocity, SIM-swap and mule
fraud and much more for agent fraud and synthetic identity, which partly supports the stated hypothesis, in the pre-specified rule's term, and refutes several of the
ranges set for it. The one variant pre-specified without burst structure is rarely caught: the diagnostic model's
recall, \nRevRecall{}, is below by point estimate (its interval reaches above it) the bound its
prediction said would mean burst structure and
counterparty novelty carry nearly all the detection power, a reading the variant's other differences
keep us from supporting as stated. Neither production model catches it at the flag
threshold. Within the variant, at the budget, the ensemble trained on \nTrainUsed{} rows (seed 1) catches \nGateRevNewBudgetCaught{} of the
\nGateRevNewBudgetRows{} rows whose recipient the account had never paid and none of the
\nGateRevKnownBudgetRows{} sent to a payee it had paid before, at the budget; the counts are small and
the split is observational, so the cause is not isolated. What survives is a methodology, the record of how each of these limits
was found, and a measurement, for one XGBoost booster in one process on one benchmark and one shared runner,
that the median cost of an exact explanation grew with the model while accuracy did not; that this cost does not depend on how easy the data is, we infer and
have not tested.

\paragraph{Future work.} The following work would turn these findings into claims about real payments.
First, validation on real, labelled transactions, under a data protection impact assessment and in a
permitted location, measuring the same floor, margins and calibration. Second, a held-out variant with
a new recipient on every row, as we read the documented form of the reversal scam, which would test
directly what the within-variant split here only suggests: that counterparty novelty is what the model
still detects once burst structure is gone. Next, why the choice of training rows moves recall at the flag threshold more than
volume does, but not recall at the budget or AUC: a test that separates the history each row carries from the months it comes from (PB-76). Last,
the serving and load measurements on dedicated hardware: the frontier at the \nPctlTail{}th
percentile, end-to-end decision latency, and scale to \nSpecRows{} rows: \deferred{M10}.
