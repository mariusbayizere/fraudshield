\begin{abstract}
What can a synthetic fraud benchmark show, and what can it not? We answer the question for
FraudShield-EAC, a seeded synthetic benchmark of East African digital payments (mobile money, USSD,
agent banking, card, online and bank transfer, across \nCountries{} countries) built to develop and
evaluate a fraud detection system. The benchmark is easy in a specific way: on the test period a single
engineered feature, a short-to-long-window velocity ratio, separates fraud from legitimate payments at
an AUC of \nGateFloor{} on its own, so we report the gate ranking metrics (AUC, recall at the false-positive budget and the per-channel AUCs) as margins over that feature rather than over chance.

Trained on all \nFvTrainRows{} rows of the training period, a calibrated XGBoost and LightGBM ensemble
reaches an AUC of \nFvAUC{}, a margin of \nFvMargin{} over the single feature. Its recall at the
specified flag threshold, \nFvRecallSix{}, misses the specified target of \nTargetRecall{}, and
training volume did not close that gap in any run we measured, from \nTrainUsed{} rows to the whole
training period. At a fixed \nTrainUsed{} rows, changing only the corpus they were drawn from (the span
of recent transactions their features are computed over, which also sets the months the rows come
from) moved mean recall at the flag threshold
across a range of \nSelMeanRange{} over \nSelDepthCount{} corpora, a range that seed noise widens by an
amount not measured, against \nLcSeedGainAC{} for a \nLcVolumeFactorAC{}-fold volume step within one
cache and \nFvSeedGainFixed{} from \nTrainUsed{} rows to the whole period on one fixed corpus (identified by the workflow that ran it), with larger
validation and calibration splits. Of the
metrics fixed in advance, this is the only one on which the corpus moved more than either volume step
did (recall at the false-positive budget: \nSelBudgetRange{} against \nLcSeedBudgetGainAC{} and
\nFvSeedBudgetGainFixed{}; AUC: \nSelAucRange{} against \nLcSeedAucGainAC{} and
\nFvSeedAucGainFixed{}). No interval was computed for these differences. These analyses were
pre-specified in repository history but are not blind: the test period had been scored at least
\nPriorReads{} times before.

On the diagnostic model, \nGroupsAboveCeiling{} disjoint feature groups each separate the classes
beyond a \nCeiling{} single-feature ceiling alone, so leave-one-group-out ablations measure redundancy
rather than importance, and leave-one-country-out and the novel fraud variant held out in time are
null. We infer, without isolating either cause, that this follows from fraud parameters not keyed by
country and from the novel variant's multi-row shape; the country holdout also shares its feature
computation and target encodings with the held-out country, an effect not measured. Refitting the
full-volume model without one fraud type at a time, which measures never fitting on a type rather
than never seeing it, costs little recall at a \nFPRBudget{} false-positive budget for velocity,
SIM-swap and mule fraud and much more for agent fraud (\nLoAgentProd{} to \nLoAgentHeld{}) and
synthetic identity, with account takeover, card-not-present and merchant fraud in between.

The one variant pre-specified in repository history has no burst structure (a single transfer, not an
incident spanning several fraudulent transactions on one account), and it is the one failure found on a variant
held out of training. It is modelled on a scam documented among Kenyan mobile-money users but sent to a payee already
on the victim's list, a modelling choice the cited sources do not support, and it was committed, with
the diagnostic model's predicted recall, alongside the generator code that produces it, before its
first committed evidence. The diagnostic model catches it at \nRevRecall{} at the \nFPRBudget{} false-positive budget: by point estimate (its
interval reaches above it) below the bound its prediction said would mean burst structure and
counterparty novelty carry nearly all the detection power, a reading the variant's other differences
keep us from supporting as stated. Neither production model catches it at the flag threshold. At a
\nFPRBudget{} false-positive budget the declared gate model (trained on \nTrainUsed{} rows, seed 1) catches
\nGateRevNewBudgetCaught{} of its \nGateRevNewBudgetRows{} rows whose recipient the account had never
paid (\nFvRevNewBudgetCaught{} at full volume) and none of the \nGateRevKnownBudgetRows{} sent to a
payee it had paid before; the counts are small and the split is observational, so the cause is not
isolated. All results are on a synthetic benchmark none of whose fraud parameters is sourced; we state
what a validation on real data would need to show.
\end{abstract}
