\begin{abstract}
We describe FraudShield, a real-time fraud detection system specified for East African digital
payments (mobile money, USSD, agent banking, card, online and bank transfer), and FraudShield-EAC,
the seeded synthetic benchmark built to develop and evaluate it. The benchmark is the paper's
subject as much as the system is: we report what it was built to support and, with the
measurements that establish them, what it cannot support.

On this benchmark a single engineered feature, the one-hour to thirty-day velocity ratio, reaches
a class separation of \nFloorM{} on its own, so we report every model metric as a margin over
that single-feature floor rather than over chance. The calibrated XGBoost and LightGBM ensemble
reaches an AUC of \nGateAUC{} \nGateAUCci{}, a margin of \nGateMargin{} over the floor measured
on the same test rows. Four disjoint feature groups each reach at least \nGroupGeographic{} alone,
so ablations on this benchmark measure redundancy, not importance. Both generalisation
experiments we planned (leave-one-country-out and a novel fraud variant held out in time) are
null, for one reason: the generator encodes fraud as bursts, and every country and variant is a
view of that structure. A pre-registered, typology-grounded variant without burst structure, a
single victim-initiated transfer to an established counterparty, is the one genuine failure we
found: it is caught at \nRevRecall{} \nRevCI{} against \nBaseVarRecall{} \nBaseVarCI{} for the
base scenarios. The most robust quantitative result is a cost the benchmark cannot weaken: across
tree complexity, the median cost of an exact TreeSHAP explanation grows about
\nFrontRatio-fold while accuracy stays within its interval.

All results are on a synthetic benchmark whose fraud parameters are modelling choices, not
observations; we state what real-data validation would need to show.
\end{abstract}
