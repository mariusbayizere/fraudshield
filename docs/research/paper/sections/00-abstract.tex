\begin{abstract}
What can a synthetic fraud benchmark show, and what can it not? We answer the question for
FraudShield-EAC, a seeded synthetic benchmark of East African digital payments (mobile money,
USSD, agent banking, card, online and bank transfer, across \nCountries{} countries) built to develop
and evaluate a fraud detection system. Its clearest negative result is a pre-registered variant built
on a scam documented among Kenyan mobile-money users and modelled as a single victim-initiated
transfer to a counterparty on the victim's own contact list: a diagnostic XGBoost model catches it at
\nRevRecall{} \nRevCI{}, against \nBaseVarRecall{} \nBaseVarCI{} for the base scenarios. That the
documented scam pays a known counterparty is our modelling assumption, not something the sources
state, and why the model misses the variant is inferred, not measured.

The benchmark is easy in a specific way. On the evaluation test rows the strongest single engineered
feature, a short-to-long-window velocity ratio, separates the classes at \nGateFloor{} on its own, so
every gate ranking metric is reported as a margin over that floor rather than over chance: trained on
\nTrainUsed{} rows, the calibrated XGBoost and LightGBM ensemble reaches an AUC of \nGateAUC{}
\nGateAUCci{}, a margin of \nGateMargin{}. Its recall at the specified flag threshold misses the
target, and \nLcVolumeFactor{} times the training data does not close the gap for this model on this
draw. \nGroupsAboveCeilingCap{} disjoint feature groups each separate the classes beyond the
\nCeiling{} single-feature ceiling alone, so ablations here measure redundancy rather than
importance, and both generalisation experiments we planned (leave-one-country-out and a novel fraud
variant held out in time) are null. We infer, without an experiment that isolates it, that both
nulls follow from the generator's burst-structured fraud. On a development laptop, the difference
between the median latency with and without an exact TreeSHAP explanation grows about
\nFrontRatio-fold across the tree configurations tested, while accuracy stays within its interval.

All results are on a synthetic benchmark whose fraud parameters are modelling choices, not
observations; we state what a validation on real data would need to show.
\end{abstract}
