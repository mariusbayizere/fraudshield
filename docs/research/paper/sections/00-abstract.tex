\begin{abstract}
What can a synthetic fraud benchmark show, and what can it not? We answer the question for
FraudShield-EAC, a seeded synthetic benchmark of East African digital payments (mobile money, USSD,
agent banking, card, online and bank transfer, across \nCountries{} countries) built to develop and
evaluate a fraud detection system. The benchmark is easy in a specific way: on the test period a single
engineered feature, a short-to-long-window velocity ratio, separates fraud from legitimate payments at
an AUC of \nGateFloor{} on its own, so we report every ranking metric of the production model as a margin over that feature
rather than over chance.

Trained on all \nFvTrainRows{} rows of the training period, a calibrated XGBoost and LightGBM ensemble
reaches an AUC of \nFvAUC{}, a margin of \nFvMargin{} over the single feature. Its recall at the
specified flag threshold, \nFvRecallSix{}, misses the specified target of \nTargetRecall{}, and
training volume did not close that gap in any run we measured, from \nTrainUsed{} rows to the whole
training period. At a fixed \nTrainUsed{} rows, changing only the corpus they were drawn from (the span of recent
transactions their features are computed over) moved mean recall at the flag threshold across a range
of \nSelMeanRange{}, against \nLcSeedGainAC{} for a \nLcVolumeFactorAC{}-fold volume step within one
cache and \nFvSeedGainFixed{} from \nTrainUsed{} rows to the whole period on one fixed corpus; of the
metrics fixed in advance, this is the only one on which the corpus moved more than either volume step
did (recall at the false-positive budget: \nSelBudgetRange{} against \nLcSeedBudgetGainAC{} and
\nFvSeedBudgetGainFixed{}; AUC: \nSelAucRange{} against \nLcSeedAucGainAC{} and \nFvSeedAucGainFixed{}). No interval was
computed for these differences.
On the diagnostic model, \nGroupsAboveCeiling{} disjoint feature groups each separate the classes beyond
a \nCeiling{} single-feature ceiling alone, so leave-one-group-out ablations measure redundancy rather than importance, and
leave-one-country-out and a fraud variant held out in time are null; we infer, without isolating either cause, that this follows from fraud
parameters not keyed by country and from the variant's multi-row shape. Refitting without one fraud
type at a time costs little recall at a \nFPRBudget{} false-positive budget for velocity, SIM-swap and mule fraud and much more
for agent fraud (\nLoAgentProd{} to \nLoAgentHeld{}) and synthetic identity.

The one variant pre-specified in repository history has no burst structure (a single transfer, not
an incident spanning several fraudulent transactions on one account), and it is the one detection
failure found. It is modelled on a scam documented among Kenyan mobile-money users but sent to a payee
already on the victim's list, a modelling choice the cited sources do not support, and it was
committed, with the diagnostic model's predicted recall, alongside the generator code that produces
it, before its evidence. The diagnostic model catches it at \nRevRecall{}, below the floor its
prediction said would mean burst structure and counterparty novelty carry nearly all the detection
power, a reading the variant's other differences keep us from supporting as stated; neither production model catches it at the flag threshold. At a \nFPRBudget{} false-positive budget the ensemble
trained on \nTrainUsed{} rows catches \nGateRevNewBudgetCaught{} of its \nGateRevNewBudgetRows{} rows whose recipient the account
had never paid (\nFvRevNewBudgetCaught{} at full volume) and none of the \nGateRevKnownBudgetRows{}
sent to a payee it had paid before; the
counts are small and the split is observational, so the cause is not isolated. All results are on a synthetic benchmark none of whose fraud
parameters is sourced; we state what a validation on real data would
need to show.
\end{abstract}
