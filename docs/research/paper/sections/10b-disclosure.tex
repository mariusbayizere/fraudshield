\section{Disclosure: how this work was produced}
\label{sec:disclosure}

\paragraph{Authorship.} This work has a sole author, who used AI coding agents (Claude Code,
Anthropic) for implementation, for review and for drafting, this paper included. The agents wrote
the generator, the feature pipeline, the models, the evaluation code, the evidence scripts and the
first drafts of the text. The author directed the work: the author supplied the governing build
instructions (\texttt{docs/prompts/FraudShield\_Master\_Build\_Prompt.md}), set the evidence rules
this paper follows, chose what to build and measure, and made every decision the repository records
as an owner decision, including what to cut, what to keep, and which findings to publish. The author
is responsible for every claim in the paper. The design choices the paper calls ``ours'', such as
the known-counterparty reading of the reversal scam (Section~\ref{sec:generalisation}), are the
author's decisions, whichever party first proposed them.

\paragraph{The commit history does not show who wrote what.} The build instructions require every
commit to be authored solely in the author's name, with no co-author trailer or tool signature
(their identity and access rules), and the repository's commit-message check rejects such trailers. A reader therefore
cannot tell from the history which lines an agent wrote and which the author wrote. This paper does
not claim that any particular part was written without an agent.

\paragraph{What the reviews were.} By the author's account, every review this paper calls
independent, and every review on record in the repository (\texttt{docs/reviews/}), was carried out by
an AI agent, not by a person; the external review of a draft, described below, is the exception whose
reviewer is not recorded. Some records say so themselves (the M4 and M5 milestone reviews, the M7
staff-authentication review); the others say only that the reviewer was independent or wrote none of
the work, so for them the attribution rests on the author's word. The author did raise individual
findings inside some reviews (for example finding CR-19 of the M1 contracts review). The build
instructions define a ``Principal Reviewer'' role, separate from the engineering roles, that assumes
the work is wrong, re-runs every check rather than re-reading it, breaks the implementation on
purpose to show the tests fail (mutation spot checks), and can block a merge. The agent took that
role in either of the following ways:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The same agent that wrote the work}, switching into the role. The M4 milestone review
  says so in its own text: it was run by the agent that wrote much of M4, including the reversal-scam
  variant, and records that this does not substitute for a reviewer who did not write it.
  \item \textbf{A fresh agent instance}, by the author's account with no access to the authoring
  session's context, reading the
  diff, the requirement and the tests, and re-running the checks itself. The M5 principal review and
  the M7 merge review state that their reviewer wrote none of the reviewed work. Apart from the external review described below, the reviews of this
  paper recorded in \texttt{REVIEW.md} and \texttt{docs/parallel/M11\_updates.md} were run by
  agents; the records do not say, for each, whether the agent shared the authoring session's
  context, and not every change to the paper has a recorded review.
\end{itemize}
Neither kind is independent in the sense a human second reader would be. By the author's account a fresh
instance shares nothing with the author's session except the repository, but it is the same kind of model working
from the same instructions, and it can share the authoring agent's blind spots. The reviews caught
real defects, among them training--serving skew in the served model, found by the M5 principal
review (\texttt{docs/reviews/M5/principal-review.md}); its findings and their fixes are in the
repository. The reviews are evidence that a check was run, not that a qualified person agreed with
the result. A further, external review of a draft of this paper prompted its latest revision; the
repository records that review and its findings (\texttt{REVIEW.md}) but not who carried it out, and
it records no other review by a person other than the author.

\paragraph{What follows for the reader.} Every number in this paper is checked mechanically against
its evidence file (Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}), and that check does not depend on who wrote the
number. The judgements are a different matter: what was measured, how it was interpreted, and which
limitations were named. They were made by the author with agent assistance and reviewed by agents,
with findings occasionally raised by the author, and by the external review of a draft whose reviewer
the repository does not record. A reader should weigh them on that basis.
