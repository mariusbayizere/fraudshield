\section{Fraud scenarios and variants}
\label{app:scenarios}

The eight scenarios and their main signals, as the scenario design notes give them
(\texttt{docs/\allowbreak ml/\allowbreak scenarios/}), with the fraud rows each contributes to the draw \texttt{d8083dbc}
(Table~\ref{tab:scenarios}). The notes describe what a detection system can observe, not how to
commit fraud. Every behavioural parameter of the scenarios is assumed
(\texttt{dataset/\allowbreak params\_provenance.md}); none is taken from a published East African fraud
statistic.

Shared by every scenario: fraud rows go through the same code path, formats and null rules as
legitimate rows, and incidents follow the victim's daily activity profile, with a share at night.

\paragraph{Variants.} Three variants are scored separately in Section~\ref{sec:generalisation}.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{adapted\_below\_threshold}: from the thirteenth simulated month, some amounts adapt
  to just below a published rule threshold. It is present in training and test.
  \item \texttt{novel\_esim\_delayed\_drain}: a SIM-swap sub-variant whose drain follows the
  enabling SIM event by days rather than minutes; \nNovelRows{} rows, test period only.
  \item \texttt{reversal\_scam\_social\_engineering}: the pre-registered non-burst variant
  (ADR 0028); \nRevRows{} rows, test period only, generated as \texttt{mule\_account} rows and
  paid to a prior contact of the victim. The battery scores \nRevFraud{} of them as fraud: the
  other \nRevScoredUnlabelled{} are fraud whose observed label says legitimate, the benchmark's
  missed-fraud noise (Section~\ref{sec:generalisation}).
\end{itemize}
