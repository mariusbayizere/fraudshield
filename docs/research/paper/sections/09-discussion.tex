\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The project kept a lab notebook (\texttt{docs/research/lab\_notebook.md}) to which defects,
corrections and predictions are appended as they happen, with later corrections written as new
entries. The rule is not absolute in practice: the notebook's header records that entries before the
dataset milestone were backfilled from earlier records, and its history shows some earlier entries
amended in place. This section is built from it. Its value is not that the benchmark is good, but
that the ways a careful process still went wrong are recorded, and some recur.

\subsection{Recurring failure patterns}

\paragraph{A control measured on the inputs is not a control on the system.} The pattern the
notebook records most often, under several names (``a guard with two doors'', ``a fix that keeps the
old behaviour as a floor''). A parameter digest guarded the generator's inputs while the claim lived
in its output: changing a constant in the generator's code re-draws the whole dataset with every
parameter value identical, which is why datasets are now identified by a fingerprint over their
output. A statistical band kept a fixed minimum width that, at release scale, was \nBandWider{} times wider
than the band it was meant to bound, so the check was blind to a construction leak landing between
about \nBandBlindLow{} and \nBandBlindHigh{}. Out-of-fold encoding removed each row's own label but
left the other rows of the same fraud incident in the estimate; grouping folds by account lowered the
single-column AUC of the strongest dataset column by \nSiblingLeak{}, and of the other columns
measured by \nSiblingLeakCurrency{} to \nSiblingLeakChannel{}. Later, account grouping turned out not
to protect features whose unit of history is a counterparty or a map cell, and the control adopted
for those is temporal: out-of-fold encodings are computed only from rows earlier than the row being
encoded. The single-column separation ceiling was true of the columns it measured and not of the
engineered features it was quoted about. The fingerprint itself first read \nFingerprintColsRead{}
of the \nFingerprintColsTotal{} columns, and it still reads a fixed sample of rows rather than every
row (Section~\ref{sec:dataset}). In each case the obvious form of the defect (the individual value,
the row) was fixed and the aggregate form (the set, the group, the regime) was not. The habit the
notebook draws from this, as a practice rather than an enforced check: for every guard, name the
object it ranges over and read the sentence it is supposed to justify; if the nouns differ, it does
not justify the sentence.

\paragraph{An asserted protection is worse than a missing one.} A note on the map-cell feature
claimed a cross-account control that did not in fact control anything; the notebook records that the
author found it while defining the contract's \texttt{history\_key} field, and the registry check
described below was added in response. A code comment explaining why a truncated corpus could not
bias a feature was a finding of the feature milestone's (M3) review. The
comment's argument turned out to be right for the feature it named, and was only established once it
was measured (Section~\ref{sec:floor}); the search it prompted found \nFurtherFeatures{} further
features with the flaw the comment had ruled out for its own. The registry now refuses a
cross-account control on a feature whose history is not keyed by account unless the control's text
contains the word ``mutation'', a check on the wording rather than on the mutation; the wider rule recorded, that an aggregate over a window can
be derived from a corpus while a statement about all of history cannot, is enforced by a test that
truncates the corpus and requires no unbounded feature to change.

\paragraph{A test that cannot fail proves nothing.} Fixtures built to be small rather than to
contain the condition under test passed vacuously: a fold test whose categories were pure, a drift
test whose fixture had no drifting rows. The rules recorded in response, that tests assert their
precondition before their conclusion and that fixtures are specified by the property they must have
before their size, are written rules; whether a test meets them is asserted by its author, not
checked.

The same defect appeared one level up, in a concurrency fix to the staff-identity service that
passed its test without exercising the case it was written for; Appendix~\ref{app:engineering}
gives the account.

\paragraph{The wrong denominator.} The central methodological error of the dataset milestone was
standardising a detector's offset against a theoretical null rather than the observed seed-to-seed
spread: a single reading of $t = \nDetectorT$ turned out to be noise once repeated seeds were run.
The first model margin reported subtracted a feature measured on \nMarginRows{} rows holding
\nMarginFraud{} confirmed frauds from a model measured on the whole sample. The evaluation report now
refuses to subtract a partial-coverage feature from a model, and a test checks that it does.

\paragraph{Records that run ahead of the facts.} A status table marked a gate as met by citing a
run that did not exist; an evidence file attributed to a commit had been produced from a dirty
working tree; a milestone tag was placed on a commit whose continuous integration had not passed.
Each is a hand-written summary of a machine-checkable fact, drifting in the flattering direction.
The status table is now generated from the existence of evidence artefacts, though some of its rows
remain asserted by the author; evidence files produced since the stamp was introduced carry the tree
state they were produced from; and the rule that a tag requires a green run on the exact commit is
written, not enforced.

\paragraph{Knowing a failure mode does not immunise against it.} Some of these patterns recurred
after they had been analysed and recorded. The lesson the notebook draws is that a defence that
depends on remembering a rule at the moment of temptation is not a defence, and that each lesson is
useful only with the question ``what would enforce this?'' attached. By that standard the record is
mixed: of the rules above, the partial-coverage refusal, the cross-account control check, the
truncation test, the generated status table and the evidence stamp are enforced by code, and the
rest are written rules.

\paragraph{A hedge is often an unmeasured claim.} The caveat ``the delay channel might be mostly an
artefact'' turned out to be measurable, and measuring it replaced a hedge with a result: about
\nEventDelayShare{} of the delay channel's separation above chance is attributed to the old schedule
(Section~\ref{sec:dataset}). The test for keeping a
caveat is to ask what measurement would remove it.

\subsection{Which rows, not only how many}
\label{sec:rowselection}

The learning curve of Section~\ref{sec:volume} found something it was not designed to find. Its
smallest point and the declared gate run both train on \nTrainUsed{} rows and are scored on the same
test rows, by the same model at the same thresholds; they differ in the corpus their features are
computed from. The gate's come from a \nGateCorpus{}-row corpus, the curve's from a
\nLcCorpusSmall{}-row one, so the training rows differ, each training row's features are computed over
a different amount of that account's past, and even the shared validation rows, which drive early
stopping, carry different values in some features (PB-76). At seed 1, recall at the flag threshold is
\nLcRecallA{} \nLcRecallciA{} for the curve's rows and \nGateRecallSix{} \nGateRecallSixci{} for the
gate's, a difference of \nSelGapSeedOne{}, while the curve's points, spanning an
\nLcVolumeFactor{}-fold change in volume, cover a range of \nLcSpanSeedOne{}. At seed 1, which rows
were used moved recall more than how many.

\textbf{This gate-against-curve comparison does not hold on the means over seeds}, and we report it
at seed 1 only. Over
\nSeeds{} seeds the curve's smallest point reads $\nLcSeedRecallA$ and the gate $\nGateSeedRecallSix$:
a difference of \nSelGapMeans{}, smaller than either standard deviation and smaller than the
\nLcSeedGain{} the mean over seeds gains across the curve. The seed-1 difference may be in part a
property of seed 1.

\textbf{The mechanism is inferred, not measured.} The corpora reach different depths, and
features that summarise an account's past are computed over less of it where the corpus is
shallower. The features PB-76 found to differ between the gate's cache and the curve's on the shared validation rows
are \texttt{amount\_to\_max\_90d\_ratio}, \texttt{amount\_zscore\_90d},
\texttt{counterparty\_confirmed\_fraud\_90d} and \texttt{distance\_from\_home\_centroid\_km}.

\textbf{The like-for-like test.} To see whether the selection matters beyond seed 1, the same test
rows, model and seeds and the number of training rows (\nTrainUsed{}) were held fixed and only the
corpus depth was varied, so that the training rows are spread over a different stretch of the training period and
carry a different amount of history (\texttt{evidence/\allowbreak round2\_\allowbreak selection\_\allowbreak d8083dbc.txt};
pre-specified in repository history, not blind to the test period). The run checked that the
recomputed training rows matched each cache's, that the test rows were identical at every depth, and
that the gate's own corpus depth reproduced the gate's \nGateRecallSix{}. Table~\ref{tab:selection}
gives the result. \textbf{Where the corpus is held fixed, we infer that which training rows are used moved recall
at the flag threshold more than how many}: the mean over \nSeeds{} seeds ranges by \nSelMeanRange{}
across corpus depths at a fixed \nTrainUsed{} training rows, against a gain of \nLcSeedGainAC{}
across the \nLcVolumeFactorAC{}-fold step of the learning curve within one cache and of
\nFvSeedGainFixed{} from
\nTrainUsed{} rows to full volume on the whole-draw corpus, where the validation and calibration
splits grow too. The full-volume run's evidence file names its feature cache only by path, so
that the run read the cache built from the whole draw rests on the workflow that passed the cache
from its build to that run, not on the evidence file. Comparisons that change the corpus and the volume together are not used, since
they mix corpus depth and training volume. No interval was computed for any of these differences, and a range over
\nSelDepthCount{} means, each with a seed standard deviation up to \nSelMaxSd{}, is widened by seed
noise by an amount not measured. The effect is not monotone in history: the shallowest corpus gives neither the lowest
nor the highest mean, so we infer that the amount of history alone does not account for it. A deeper corpus both
gives each row more history and spreads the rows over earlier months, and this test does not separate
these effects; the mechanism above remains an inference.

What follows for this paper is a qualification, not a correction: every single-number result here
measured at one selection of training rows, the gate figures of Table~\ref{tab:gate} included, is a

\subsection{Predictions pre-specified in repository history}

The lab notebook records falsifiable predictions that it says were written before the evidence.
The notebook is the author's own file: some entries were back-filled and some amended in place, so
its word is not evidence of when a prediction was made, and nothing here is pre-registration in the
usual sense, an analysis plan posted to an independent registry before the research outcomes are
observed \cite{nosek2018preregistration}.
What the repository can show is narrower. If the commit that first contains a prediction is an
ancestor of the commit that first contains its evidence, the prediction was committed before the
evidence was. That is all it shows: it cannot show that the evidence had not already been seen when
the prediction was written, and commits are ordered on the author's machine, so a history rewritten
before it was pushed would look the same. Table~\ref{tab:predictions} lists the \nPredTotal{}
predictions whose outcome the notebook records, and \nPredPb{} of PB-66's own ranges that its run did
not confirm in full (the owner's hypothesis for leave-one-fraud-type-out is reported in
Section~\ref{sec:lofto});
PB-66's other predictions, for full volume, precision-recall, the larger budgets, the months, the cost
curve and the fairness report, held, and its pre-specification lists them
(\texttt{docs/research/preregistration\_pb66\_full\_volume.md}). For \nPrespecShown{} of them git orders the
prediction before its evidence; for the other \nPrespecNotShown{} it does not establish the order,
and they are not counted as pre-specified. One of those, the learning-curve prediction, does sit
before its evidence in the history, but both commits were rebased on 2026-10-18, after the outcome was
known, so that order was written after the fact and shows nothing. Every commit pair and date in the
table (UTC) is checked by \texttt{audit\_numbers.py} on every build: an ordered pair must be a strict
ancestor relation, an unordered one must not be, and a rebased pair must have been rewritten after its
evidence's author date. The entry that recorded the takeover-lead prediction also recorded others
whose outcomes are not reported there; they are not counted.

The later analyses were specified the same way, in commits that precede their evidence: PB-66's
full-volume run (\prespec{da3a492}{2026-10-19}{c61d512}{2026-10-20}), its alert budget of
\nBudKA{} a day and that budget's prediction (\prespec{1eeba04}{2026-10-19}{c61d512}{2026-10-20}), its
leave-one-fraud-type-out ranges (\prespec{eeccd60}{2026-10-19}{c61d512}{2026-10-20}), and this
paper's second round of evidence (\prespec{74ea29a}{2026-10-19}{f29793c}{2026-10-20}). One part of
that round was written later: the code that reads PB-66's per-row scores was committed before those
scores were read, but after the round's other results were known. None of these analyses is blind:
the test period had been scored \nPriorReads{} times before PB-66's run.

For the \nPrespecShown{} pre-specified predictions the history supports this much: they were committed
before their evidence, and their outcomes, most of them refutations, are those in the table. It
does not support a claim that the author predicted well. For the other \nPrespecNotShown{}, the
notebook says they were written first and the commits cannot confirm it: the learning-curve
prediction was rebased after its outcome, and the rest were committed with or after their evidence. The
notebook's own register of its earliest predictions, which said all of them preceded their evidence,
is corrected by a later notebook entry rather than in place. The notebook credits those early
refutations with a standing rule, the feature contract's \texttt{history\_key} field and E1's density
threshold for component folding; the density threshold was itself found, the same day, to rule
component folding out for most history keys, and the control adopted is the temporal one described
above.
