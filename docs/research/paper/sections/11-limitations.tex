\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

We state these as findings about what the evidence supports, not as disclaimers.

\paragraph{The benchmark is synthetic, and the fraud in it is invented.} It does not support statements about
real fraud rates, real detection performance or any institution's customers. Whether it supports
comparisons between methods is not established either: Section~\ref{sec:rowselection} shows a
choice of training rows moving a headline metric at seed 1, and no comparison of methods here
repeats over selections. Every figure here is a property of this
generator.

\paragraph{No fraud parameter is sourced.} Of \nProvFraudTotal{} fraud parameters,
\nProvFraudAssumed{} are modelling choices and \nProvFraudCalibrated{} are calibrated to
specification targets. No publication read gives fraud incidence by type for these markets. Any
result that depends on the level or mix of fraud inherits this assumption. The sourced parameters (\nProvSourced{} of \nProvTotal{}) fix
population and volume structure, partly from a neighbouring market's statistics.

\paragraph{The benchmark is easily separable, we infer by its incident structure.} Several of its scenarios stage
fraud as multi-row incidents. A single velocity feature reaches \nGateFloor{} on the test rows,
\nGroupsAboveCeiling{} feature groups each exceed the single-feature ceiling alone,
leave-one-country-out and the novel variant are both null, and the one pre-specified variant
without that structure is caught at \nRevRecall{}. We infer that the separability and the novel-variant null follow from
the incident structure, and the country null from fraud parameters that are not keyed by country; no
experiment varies either. Detection of this benchmark's own scenarios is what it measures;
nothing here speaks to fraud with other structure, except as the failure in
Section~\ref{sec:generalisation}. The specification's distribution targets (overall and test-period
fraud rate, channel and country mix) are requirements met by construction, not observations.

\paragraph{Features with no source data, and one resting on assumed values.} Of the \nFeatures{}
features, \nNoSourceFeatures{}, about \nNoSourceShare{}, read data this benchmark does not contain and
are missing for every record: \texttt{account\_age\_days} and
\texttt{counterparty\_account\_age\_days} need account opening dates, held in an institution's account
master; \texttt{kyc\_tier} needs the account's KYC tier assignments with the time each took effect,
held in its KYC records; \texttt{agent\_float\_utilisation\_ratio} needs the agent's float balance and
limit, and \texttt{agent\_distance\_from\_registered\_km} the agent's registered premises, both held by
the agent network. The models learn nothing from them, and with one feature that is constant on this
benchmark they are why the models train on \nTrainableFeatures{} features (Appendix~\ref{app:features}).
\textbf{No model in this paper learned from these features}: the full models of the gate, the
diagnostic battery and the learning curve train on \nTrainableFeatures{}; the specification's requirement that the features be computable for at least
\nComputableTarget{} of records is recorded as not met (ADR 0055). One more feature is a different limitation. \texttt{round\_sum\_flag} produces a
value for every record, but only because each country's configuration supplies the currency's round
denominations, and those values are \textbf{assumed}: plausible note and typing steps, not a banknote
series read from any central-bank source. The feature's input is something this project wrote, not
something it observed. The gap is the benchmark's rather than the design's: the \nNoSourceFeatures{} features are
implemented and tested on both computation paths, and they are designed to take their inputs from an institution's
account master, KYC records and agent ledger; that such data would supply them, and what they would
add, is not shown here.

\paragraph{No real-data validation.} None has been done. A validation on real data would need to
show, at minimum: the single-feature floor on real engineered features (whether real fraud is as
burst-separable as this benchmark's); the ensemble's margin over that floor; calibration in the
decision region; leave-one-country-out on real, country-labelled fraud; and detection of
documented non-burst typologies such as the reversal scam. It would require data access agreements,
a data protection impact assessment, and hosting in a permitted location (Section~\ref{sec:ethics}).

\paragraph{What the benchmark cannot exercise.} Cross-bloc and intercontinental transfers cannot occur
by the benchmark's configuration, so the models have never scored one (Section~\ref{sec:setting}).
The pre-specified reversal-scam variant sends the victim's transfer to a counterparty on the
victim's payee list, a modelling choice that differs from the documented form of the scam, in which
the victim pays the scammer's own number, which we read as a new counterparty; it also differs from
the base scenarios in its amount rule, and it carries counterparty novelty on \nRevNovelScored{} of its \nRevFraud{} scored rows, so
it removes that signal from only some of them (Section~\ref{sec:generalisation}). The result says nothing about the
documented form, and does not isolate why the variant is missed. And because no accuracy advantage
of the specified ensemble over a single XGBoost model was detected here, the benchmark cannot show
what the ensemble adds.

\paragraph{Scale and volume.} Results are at \nRowsDrawB{} rows or fewer, not the \nSpecRows{} the
specification requires, a scale that is \deferred{M10}; and the models were trained on \nTrainUsed{}
rows rather than the full training period. The strongest dataset column's AUC moves with dataset size
by more than seed noise, and the cause is unexplained; every figure is therefore stated at its scale.
The \nGateFailed{} failing gate metrics
(recall and false-negative rate at the flag threshold) do not close with more training data from
\nLcTrainA{} to \nLcTrainD{} rows (Section~\ref{sec:volume}); beyond that volume they are untested.

\paragraph{One selection of training rows.} At \nTrainUsed{} rows, a different selection of training
rows from a corpus of different depth read recall \nSelGapSeedOne{} higher at seed 1, more than an
\nLcVolumeFactor{}-fold change in volume moved it; on the means over seeds the difference is \nSelGapMeans{}, within
the seed spread (Section~\ref{sec:rowselection}). Only the gate's selection and the curve's exist, so
how much a headline
figure varies with the choice of training rows is unmeasured, and every such figure here is a
measurement of one selection.

\paragraph{A ranking metric can hide a change in decisions.} The scoring service as built (milestone
M5) serves \nSkewConstantFeatures{} of the trained features as constants, because nothing deployed yet
writes the state they read: the counterparty's confirmed-fraud count, the map cell's lagged fraud
rate and the days since a SIM swap; the synthetic-identity score is computed from fewer of its terms
(ADR 0034). With the gate model rebuilt at seed 1 and scored over the whole test period on the
features as served, AUC falls from \nSkewAucTrained{} to \nSkewAucBoth{}, by \nSkewAucDrop{}: more
than the half-width of the gate's interval (\nSkewHalfWidth{}) and less than its full width, a change
no AUC-expressed gate here would refuse. The decisions change more: the frauds reaching the flag
threshold fall from \nSkewFlagTrained{} to \nSkewFlagBoth{}, \nSkewLost{} fewer (\nSkewLostPct{}),
and \nSkewTierBoth{} risk-tier assignments change. The missing outcomes account for most of it (AUC
\nSkewAucOutcomes{}, \nSkewFlagOutcomes{} frauds flagged), the missing SIM swaps for less
(\nSkewAucSim{}, \nSkewFlagSim{}). These figures come from ADR 0034 and the scoring milestone's
re-review by an agent that had written neither the code nor the earlier review
(Section~\ref{sec:disclosure}), which reproduced every row; no raw output file with a provenance stamp was
committed for them, so their source is a review record rather than an evidence file. They describe
what the served feature set does to the evaluated model on the test period, one model at one
threshold. No deployment has run, so they are not a measured loss of detection in operation, and they
are not a general property of AUC. Our own gate would have passed this deployment, which is why the
serving path carries its own acceptance test, and why M10 is required to re-measure the skew and find
none (PB-72).

\paragraph{The verification effort was blind in one direction.} The scoring client was reviewed
\nFakeReviews{} times (a principal review, a re-review of its fixes, a verification pass and an
adversarial pass over the promotion path, each by an agent), and the reviewers found \nFakeDefects{} defects in the
model-promotion path between them, while \nFakeTests{} tests passed. None found that the client
misread how the model registry reports an unset alias, because every one of those tests ran against an
in-process fake that encoded the same misreading; a test that starts a real registry container
caught it in continuous integration. The figures and the failure are recorded in the lab notebook
(2026-09-23); no raw output was committed beyond that record. Review, mutation testing and
adversarial probing all operate inside the world the test fixtures define; contact with the real
dependency is the only check outside it. The verification reported for this system therefore does
not establish correctness against the external services it integrates with, beyond the tests that
start real ones, and the scoring milestone's re-review records that some of those had not run on any
machine visible to it.

\paragraph{Latency.} No latency figure here is a gate figure. The frontier is a difference of
medians on a shared development laptop, whose measured \nPctlTail{}th percentile was not credible; serving and
end-to-end latencies have been measured only on shared development hardware (ADR 0035, ADR 0059),
and the gate measurement is \deferred{M10}.

\paragraph{Intervals assume independence the data do not have.} Fraud on this benchmark is staged as
multi-row incidents on one account, and an account's rows share its history, so test rows are not
independent. For the gate metrics, the diagnostic model's group AUCs and the variant recalls, an
account-clustered bootstrap from a reproduction of both models is reported beside the published
intervals; it widens them little except for USSD's AUC (Table~\ref{tab:clustered}), and the novel
variant's \nNovelRows{} rows rest on \nNovelAccounts{} accounts. Every other interval in the paper
(the learning curve, leave-one-country-out, the single-feature floor, the baselines' DeLong tests, the
frontier and the earlier-draw battery) treats rows as independent and was not re-computed, so each is
narrower than it should be by an amount not measured, and comparisons that rest on those intervals
not overlapping are weaker than they read.

\paragraph{The diagnostic model is untuned, and overfit where it was measured.} Its overfitting was measured on the velocity
group alone and is inferred, not measured, for the others: on the velocity group its test AUC
peaks early and declines, ending below the group's own strongest feature
(Section~\ref{sec:redundancy}). Every group-alone and leave-one-group-out figure comes from it, so
the group figures are lower bounds and no ranking of groups by importance is supported.

\paragraph{Small counts.} Variant, country and channel results rest on between \nAdaptFraud{} and
\nBaseVarFraud{} fraud rows; intervals are reported for the variant recalls and in the evidence files for the channel and
country AUCs, and the evidence file marks the
Democratic Republic of the Congo's, which rest on fewer than \nSmallCountThreshold{}, as direction
only.

\paragraph{Figures that depend on the run.} Figures moved between the earlier battery and the
evaluated one, which differ in draw, encoding and held-out rows, among them the redundancy threshold,
the leave-one-country-out loss and the variant recalls; the findings reported here did not change.
Each figure is stated with the run it comes from. The single-feature floor of
Section~\ref{sec:floor} comes from a draw whose fingerprint was not recorded.

\paragraph{Claims not made.} The specification's figures on regional fraud growth, payment volume,
analyst dismissal rates, the accuracy of models built elsewhere on East African data, and annual model
degradation are unverified or replaced in the project's claims register, the claimed ensemble
variance reduction is not supported by the measurement of Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}, and none is
stated here as fact.
