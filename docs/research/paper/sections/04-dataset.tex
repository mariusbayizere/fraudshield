\section{The FraudShield-EAC benchmark}
\label{sec:dataset}

\subsection{Generator}

FraudShield-EAC is produced by an agent-based, seeded simulator. Its entities are customers,
accounts, devices (absent for USSD-only users), SIM events, agents, merchants, and counterparties.
Each customer's home location is drawn from a single cluster around one centre per country (an
assumed spread; district population weights are not used), and the customer's KYC tier is internal
to the generator and is not in any released table. Agents have no float and no registered premises
in the generated data (ADR 0055). Legitimate behaviour includes salary cycles, market days,
school-fee periods, family remittances along EAC corridors, round-sum transfers, and agent cash-in
and cash-out peaks; volume grows over \nMonths{} simulated months. \nScenariosCap{} fraud scenarios
are injected, each with randomised parameters and temporal evolution: SIM-swap takeover, account
takeover through a credential or device change, agent fraud, velocity fraud, card-not-present
fraud, mule accounts (fan-in and fan-out), merchant fraud and synthetic identity. Adversarial
adaptation moves amounts just below one simulated, assumed rule threshold from the
\nAdaptMonthOrdinal{} simulated month (index \nAdaptMonthIndex{}). The test period alone also carries a delayed SIM-swap drain and, in
the evaluation draw, the pre-registered reversal scam of Section~\ref{sec:generalisation}.

Results use seed \nSeed{}, on the draws named where each is used. The model evaluation
uses draw \texttt{d8083dbc} (\nRowsDrawB{} rows), which carries the pre-registered variant of
Section~\ref{sec:generalisation}. The dataset-column measurements and the earlier battery use draw
\texttt{6abde44e} (\nRowsDrawA{} rows). The engineered single-feature measurements of
Section~\ref{sec:floor} were taken on an earlier draw of \nFloorDrawRows{} rows, at the same seed,
whose evidence file records no fingerprint; it predates \texttt{6abde44e} and the change to the
takeover lead time described below, so it is not the same data. The specification asks for at least
\nSpecRows{} rows; that scale is \notyet{M10}, and every result here is reported at its stated
scale.

\subsection{What the parameters rest on}

A sourcing pass on 2026-09-18 read \nSourceDocs{} documents in full and raised the number of
sourced generator parameters from none to \nParamsSourced{} of the \nParamsTotal{} that existed
then. The generator at the evaluated draw has \nProvTotal{} parameters: \nProvSourced{} sourced,
\nProvAssumed{} modelling choices, and \nProvCalibrated{} calibrated to targets in the
specification (\texttt{dataset/params\_provenance.md}); the count grew when country facts moved into
per-country packs (currency and its minor units, time zone, exchange rate, bloc memberships,
continent, home-location centre and round denominations). The sourced parameters rest on these documents,
each re-read for this paper:
\begin{itemize}
  \item transactions per active customer, mobile money volume growth, per-channel mean amounts and
        the person-to-person share: the Bank of Tanzania's payment systems report
        \cite{botPSAR2024}, Annex H, Tables H1, H2, H3 and H5 (page 48) and H8 (page 49), and
        Annex I (page 50);
  \item the cross-border share: the same report, Annex G, Table G2 (page 47);
  \item active customers per agent and per merchant acceptance point: the National Bank of Rwanda's
        annual report \cite{bnrAR2425}, Table 11 (page 86) and the count of active mobile payment
        subscribers (page 87);
  \item the urban share of the population: Rwanda's 2022 census \cite{nisrCensus2022},
        Table 2 (printed page 5, page 30 of the PDF), and mobile money use by urban and rural adults:
        FinScope Rwanda 2024 \cite{afrFinscope2024}, Figure 26 (page 40);
  \item exchange rates: the World Bank's official exchange rates \cite{wbFCRF}; time zones: the
        IANA time zone database \cite{ianaTZ2026c}; currency codes and minor units: the ISO 4217
        list \cite{iso4217list};
  \item the months in which school terms begin: one Rwandan school's 2024--2025 calendar
        \cite{ngaCalendar2425};
  \item regional-bloc membership: the East African Community \cite{eacOverview} and COMESA
        \cite{comesaMembers} membership pages, and for Tanzania's and the Democratic Republic of the
        Congo's memberships also the SADC member-states page, which could not be opened when the
        paper's references were checked and is not in the bibliography. These pages are not among the \nSourceDocs{}
        documents of the sourcing pass: they were read on 2026-09-19 as the sources of the
        countries' bloc memberships, and are cited in \texttt{dataset/params\_provenance.md}.
\end{itemize}
Because the Tanzanian report gives value and volume by transaction category and no Rwandan
breakdown was found in the documents read, per-transaction levels come from Tanzanian aggregates while the country mix is Rwanda-weighted;
they are order-of-magnitude anchors, not claims about one market.

\textbf{No fraud parameter is sourced.} Of the \nProvFraudTotal{} fraud parameters,
\nProvFraudAssumed{} are modelling choices (scenario prevalence and mix, incident length, burst
timing, adaptation, mule structure, the held-out variants) and \nProvFraudCalibrated{} are
calibrated to specification targets. No central bank publication read gives fraud incidence by
type for these markets; sourcing those figures from vendor reports or global aggregates presented
as regional would make the paper look better and be less honest. Every result that depends on the
\emph{level} of fraud inherits this assumption (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Realism and anti-leakage controls}

A realism report, run as a separate command and in continuous integration on a generated
dataset, exits with an error when a gated control fails; both evaluated draws pass it.
The controls, and why each exists:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Single-column separation.} No dataset column may separate the classes beyond an
        AUC of \nCeiling{} (resolution D-08). The strongest column is the merchant category, at
        \nColumnMaxAUCB{} on the evaluation draw (\nColumnMaxAUC{} on draw \texttt{6abde44e}).
        Section~\ref{sec:features} shows that the ceiling, true of the columns, is not true of the
        engineered features.
  \item \textbf{A shortcut detector} trained on non-behavioural columns (identifiers, file order,
        construction artefacts) must stay within its own null band around chance; on the
        evaluation draw it reads \nShortcutAUC{}.
  \item \textbf{Construction checks}: account events, identifier characters, file order and
        per-channel null signatures must not reveal the label. The account-event check exists
        because of a leak found in that secondary table rather than in the feature matrix: in its
        first version, \nEventLeakShare{} of events whose timestamp carried a fractional second sat on a fraud
        account, against a base rate of \nEventLeakBase{} (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).
  \item \textbf{Label noise} in both directions, measured at \nLabelNoiseMissed{} missed and
        \nLabelNoiseFalse{} false labels as a share of true fraud on the evaluation draw.
  \item \textbf{Temporal placement}: the novel sub-variant occurs only in the test period
        (\nNovelRows{} rows, none before the test start).
  \item \textbf{Distribution targets} from the specification (fraud rate, channel and country
        mix) are met by construction: overall fraud rate \nFraudRateAll{}, test-period rate
        \nFraudRateTest{}, largest channel-mix deviation \nChannelMixDev{} percentage points and
        country-mix deviation \nCountryMixDev{}. These are requirements the generator was built
        to meet, not observations about East African payments.
\end{itemize}

One reported (not gated) channel deserves its own sentence: the delay between an account event
(a SIM swap or device change) and that account's next transaction separates the classes at
\nEventDelayAUC{} on draw \texttt{6abde44e} and \nEventDelayAUCB{} on the evaluation draw. When the
takeover lead time was changed from a narrow uniform window to a
long-tailed distribution, the channel fell from \nEventDelayBefore{} to \nEventDelayAfter{},
inside a band of \nEventDelayBandLow{}--\nEventDelayBandHigh{} predicted before the regenerated
draw existed. We infer that about \nEventDelayShare{} of the channel's separation above chance was an artefact of
the uniform schedule; that attributes the whole fall to the old schedule, and the new distribution
could add separation of its own. That the remainder reflects the scenario rather than the new,
equally assumed lead distribution is not established: no published takeover-to-drain delays for
these markets were found, so that distribution is a modelling choice too.

\subsection{The minimum viable scale}

Below about \nMinRows{} rows the generator cannot stage all \nScenarios{} scenarios: the mule role pool
is usually empty, and a scenario that cannot be staged has its share reallocated to those that
can, so the fraud \emph{rate} is still met exactly while the scenario \emph{mix} is wrong. Until
this was found, runs below that size silently lacked \texttt{mule\_account}. The earlier derivations
of the minimum were wrong, and each failed the same sanity check by returning a minimum below a size
that had already failed. The derivation used solves $E[\text{holders}](n) \ge \text{needed}(n) +
\nMinSigmas\sqrt{\text{needed}(n)}$ numerically. The lab notebook reports it giving \nMinRowsA{},
\nMinRowsB{}, \nMinRowsC{} and \nMinRowsD{} rows from runs at different scales; the test that
guards it generates its own smaller runs and requires the minimum they imply to agree within a fixed
tolerance. A separate minimum, about \nDetectorMinRows{} rows, is where the shortcut detector
has full power against a planted leak; completeness binds first. The generator now refuses a run
too small to stage every scenario, and a waived development run records the missing scenarios in
its manifest.

\subsection{Split and provenance}

The split is strictly temporal (resolution D-07): on the evaluation draw, \nTrainRows{} training
rows, \nValRows{} validation rows (whose chronologically last \nCalRows{} form the calibration
split), a \nEmbargoDays{}-day embargo of \nEmbargoRows{} rows excluded to model label delay, and a
test period of \nTestRows{} rows spanning \nTestSpanDays{} days. Scoring runs on the test set are
logged (\texttt{docs/benchmarks/test\_set\_access.jsonl}); runs made before the log existed were
back-filled from committed evidence. The first label count this paper produced for
Section~\ref{sec:generalisation} read the test period without an entry; it is now back-filled like
the others, and the paper's evidence scripts record every later read themselves.

A dataset is identified by a \textbf{fingerprint over its output}, not only by a digest of its
parameters, because a draw can change with every parameter value identical. The fingerprint is a
SHA-256 over every column of a fixed sample of \nFingerprintSampleRows{} rows per table, the rows
that sort first, plus each table's row count; it is not a hash of every row. Evidence files produced
since 2026-09-21 record the commit \textbf{and the working-tree state} they were produced from;
earlier files, including the single-feature measurement of Section~\ref{sec:floor}, carry no such
stamp. The stamp exists because a hash written by hand records an intention: one earlier report, attributed to a commit, turned out to
have been produced from a dirty working tree (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).
