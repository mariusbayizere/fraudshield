# M2 walkthrough — the dataset generator

## What was built

M2 produces the benchmark the rest of the project is measured on: a synthetic East African payment
dataset whose distribution targets hold by construction, whose leakage properties are enforced by
tests, and whose every parameter says where its value came from.

1. **The generator** (`dataset/src/fraudshield_dataset/generator/`, ADR 0022):
   - 24 simulated months of transactions, labels and account events for five countries, written as
     partitioned Parquet, streamed a month at a time (184 MiB peak resident memory at a million
     rows, against a 2 GiB budget);
   - deterministic from a seed: the same seed gives byte-identical files at any batch size, verified
     including across a Parquet row-group boundary;
   - eight fraud scenarios with adversarial adaptation from month 12 and one novel sub-variant that
     appears only in the temporal hold-out test period.
2. **Targets met by construction, not by sampling luck** (`generator/config.py`, `generator/fraud.py`,
   `generator/legit.py`):
   - a calibrated monthly fraud intensity schedule, refused if the planned test period misses its
     target;
   - each month's fraud target split across scenarios by share, placed as whole incidents on exactly
     that many victims, with reallocation when a scenario cannot run yet;
   - country and segment assigned by sequential apportionment, so every prefix of the population is
     within one customer of its quota;
   - per-cell monthly row quotas, so volume and the mixes carry no per-customer sampling noise;
   - channel mixes fitted to the SRS mix by iterative proportional fitting, at any population split.
3. **Anti-leakage and realism as gates** (`realism/checks.py`, `realism/stats.py`):
   - single-feature AUC ceiling, with categorical encoding computed out of fold;
   - a shortcut detector over every transaction-id byte, the sub-second timestamp, row position and
     the label delay, grouped by account so an incident cannot straddle a fold;
   - identifier construction over every character of every token; account-event construction over
     each event's sub-second part and day of month;
   - null patterns per channel, value formats, identifier uniqueness, label noise, novelty placement;
   - every band that judges a tree is sized to that statistic's own null, and each gate has a test
     that plants its violation and fails without the fix.
4. **Provenance and release** (`dataset/generator/params/`, `release/export.py`):
   - 81 parameters, each with a value, unit, provenance, citation or rationale; 12 sourced from
     documents read in full, 14 calibrated to SRS targets, 55 modelling choices;
   - a measured influence ranking that decided which parameters were worth sourcing;
   - `fs-dataset export`: CSV written only at export, the licence and the generated documents
     alongside, and a `SHA256SUMS` a recipient can verify with one command.

## Key decisions and alternatives

- **Exact quotas over probabilistic sampling.** Meeting SRS 7.1 in expectation left a 200K-row run
  2.2 points off the country mix and 0.3 points off the test-period fraud rate. Quotas hold at any
  size; the cost is that the generator refuses parameter sets it cannot satisfy, which is the point.
- **Iterative proportional fitting for the channel mix** rather than fixing one segment's mix and
  letting the others absorb the remainder. The remainder approach broke when the sourced population
  turned out to be 68% rural.
- **Sourced facts constrain assumed ones.** The urban share of customers is sourced; the split
  between behavioural segments is not, and the generator refuses a split that contradicts the share.
- **NumPy rather than scikit-learn** for the detector and the statistics (ADR 0022 section 7): SciPy's
  wheel bundles runtime libraries under several licences, and the checks need only a rank AUC and a
  depth-limited tree.

## What the sources changed

The generator started with 79 parameters, none of them sourced. A sourcing pass
(`docs/research/sourcing_pass.md`) read eight documents in full and raised that to 12 of 81. Seven
assumed values turned out to be wrong, and two of the corrections changed the design rather than
just a number.

| Parameter | Assumed | Sourced | Source | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `population.mean_transactions_per_active_customer_month` | 14 | 8.8 | Bank of Tanzania, Payment Systems Annual Report 2024, Table H1, p47 (6,414M transactions / 60,745,698 active users / 12) | 59% more simulated customers for the same row target, so per-customer history is shorter and fraud is spread over more accounts |
| `population.customers_per_agent` | 150 | 30 | NBR Annual Report 2024-2025, Table 11, p86 (251,042 agents against 7,457,114 subscribers) | five times as many agents; agent fraud concentrates less in a few outlets |
| `population.customers_per_merchant` | 40 | 12 | NBR Annual Report 2024-2025, Table 11, p86 (625,489 modern POS) | three times as many merchants; fewer repeat visits per merchant, so merchant fraud has thinner per-merchant history |
| `population.urban_share_of_customers` | 0.45 implied by the segment split | 0.32 | NISR Census 2022, Table 2, p30 (27.9% urban) weighted by FinScope Rwanda 2024, Figure 26, p40 (87% urban and 72% rural adults use mobile money) | the customer population is mostly rural, which broke the channel-mix algebra (below) |
| `behaviour.cross_border_share` | 0.03 | 0.005 | Bank of Tanzania, Annex G Table G2 and Annex H Table H2, p47 (2,211,374 outbound cross-border wallet transfers against 479.1M person-to-person transfers) | six times fewer cross-border rows, so `counterparty_country` differs from the sender's country far less often |
| `behaviour.school_fee_months` | January, May, September | January, April, September | Rwandan school calendar 2024-2025 (term 3 begins 22-25 April) | the third seasonal volume peak moved a month earlier |
| `currencies.utc_offset_hours` (DRC) | +01:00 (Kinshasa) | +02:00 (eastern DRC) | IANA tzdata 2026c, Africa/Lubumbashi | local-hour features for Congolese rows shift by an hour; `currencies.country_centre` moved to Goma to match |

Two consequences went beyond values:

**Channel mixes are now fitted.** The old model fixed the rural segment's channel mix and let the
other segments absorb whatever the SRS mix left over. With a rural majority, that remainder pushed
urban customers to a fifth of their payments on cards and almost nothing through agents — an
artefact of the algebra, not a modelling decision. Each segment now has *relative* channel
preferences, and iterative proportional fitting scales them until the mixture is the SRS channel mix
exactly. ML-DATA-03 is met by construction at any population split, and the segment profiles stay
plausible: rural customers are USSD and agent heavy, salaried urban customers card and online heavy.

**The smartphone rewrite is gone.** The generator used to turn a smartphone owner's USSD payment
into a wallet payment. Once every segment carried some USSD, that rewriting moved the realised
channel mix 3.8 percentage points away from the fitted one. It is removed; whether a row carries a
device is decided by the channel, because a USSD session has no app device.

Re-verified after the changes, at 60,000 rows: fraud rate 0.870% overall and 0.906% in the test
period, channel mix within 0.34 pp, country mix within 0.12 pp. The calibrated SRS targets take
precedence over sourced values and still hold.

## What is still assumed

No fraud parameter is sourced: 21 of the 25 in `fraud.yaml` are ASSUMED and the other four are
calibrated to SRS targets. Most behavioural timing parameters are assumed too, though the school
terms and the cross-border share are now sourced. No central bank in the region publishes fraud
incidence by type, so scenario prevalence, incident length and attack timing are modelling choices
and are labelled as such. `docs/research/sourcing_pass.md` lists what was
searched and not found.

## Try it

```console
$ uv run fs-dataset provenance                       # regenerate the provenance table
$ uv run fs-dataset generate --output /tmp/ds --rows 200000
$ uv run fs-dataset check /tmp/ds --rows 200000      # gate checks, with confidence intervals
$ uv run fs-dataset report /tmp/ds --rows 200000     # the realism report
$ uv run fs-dataset export /tmp/ds --output /tmp/release && (cd /tmp/release && sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS)
```

## What the results mean

At 1,006,249 rows (seed 20260917, 7 minutes, 184 MiB peak):

| Property | Measured | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Fraud rate, overall | 0.870% (95% CI 0.852–0.888) | 0.87% ±0.5 pp (ML-DATA-02) |
| Fraud rate, test period | 0.905% (0.849–0.965) | 0.91% ±0.5 pp |
| Monthly fraud rate | 24 of 24 intervals cover their target | by construction |
| Channel mix | within 0.16 pp | ±0.5 pp (ML-DATA-03) |
| Country mix | within 0.01 pp | ±0.5 pp (ML-DATA-05) |
| Strongest single transaction feature | 0.711 (`merchant_category_code`) | ≤ 0.80 (D-08) |
| Strongest single channel overall | **0.758 (event-to-transaction delay, via the `account_events` join)** | ≤ 0.80 (D-08) |
| Shortcut detector | 0.510, band ±0.030 | within its null band |
| Account-event construction | 0.513, band ±0.030 | within its null band |
| Account-event type (reported, ungated) | 0.537 | ≤ 0.80 (D-08) |
| Identifier construction | 0.506 / 0.508 / 0.514 | each within ±0.030 |
| Duplicate identifiers | 0 in 1,006,249 | none |
| Rows | 1,006,249 | ≥ 5,000,000 for a release (ML-DATA-01) |

The last row is the honest limit of M2: the release-size run has not happened. What the numbers do
establish is that the targets hold by construction rather than by luck, and that the leakage
standard is enforced by tests that demonstrably fail when their property is broken.

The strongest single feature **among the transaction columns** is the merchant category, at 0.711:
category 4829 (person-to-person transfer) carries a 3.7× fraud lift, because the sourced
person-to-person share makes merchant payments the norm while fraud drains go to people. That is
designed scenario behaviour, not a construction artefact, and it is inside the D-08 ceiling.

**It is not the strongest channel in the dataset, and an earlier version of this page said it was.**
Joining `account_events` to the transactions — which the datasheet explicitly invites, since the two
share the account token — gives the time from a SIM swap or device change to that account's next
transaction. That delay separates the classes at **0.758**, above the merchant category's 0.711, and
it is the largest single signal the dataset contains. It is still inside the D-08 ceiling of 0.80,
but by 0.042 rather than comfortably.

Two things about that number are worth saying plainly rather than leaving for a reader to find.

The first is *why* it is large. A SIM swap before an account takeover is how that fraud actually
works, and a model is meant to learn it; that part is designed signal. But the lead time is drawn
from `fraud.takeover_lead_minutes = [5, 60]`, whose provenance is `ASSUMED` — so every enabling
event is followed by its drain inside a tight uniform window, with no long tail and no swap that is
never exploited. Real SIM swaps sometimes precede nothing. Part of the 0.758 is therefore the
assumed schedule rather than the phenomenon, and the two cannot be separated from the data as it
stands. That is a limitation of the generator, not a finding about fraud.

The second is *how it was missed*. Until this milestone the channel was judged by nothing at all.
The account-event gate reads only construction — the sub-second part of the timestamp and the day of
the month — and deliberately leaves out event type and delay as the scenario's own signal. The
single-feature AUC gate scores transaction columns and never joins the events table. So the two
excluded channels fell through the gap between the two gates: asserted in a docstring as "0.537 on
its own", tracked by no check, and free to drift with nothing to notice. The figure quoted was the
*type* channel, the weaker of the two; the delay channel was never measured. Both are now reported
in the realism report on every run, still ungated, so the claim is a number rather than an
assertion. The delay separation also grows with sample size — 0.709 at 60K, 0.758 at 1M — so any
future argument about it has to be made at release scale.

We found this ourselves, after the review closed, by measuring what the exclusion had excused.

## History notes (disclosures)

- An independent review of this milestone found a **BLOCKER**: fraud-planted account events were
  written to the microsecond on any day of the month while legitimate ones were quantised to whole
  seconds and capped at day 27, so the sub-second part of an event timestamp identified a victim's
  account with 99.8% precision — in a table the release ships. It is fixed, the checks now read that
  table, and the test that was supposed to cover the property (it asserted only that two event types
  exist) was rewritten. `docs/reviews/M2/dataset-response.md` records this and every other finding.
- The same review planted three leaks that passed every check, because the detectors read 2 of 16
  identifier bytes, 3 of 32 token characters, and nothing of `label_available_at`. All are read now.
- The committed realism report described a superseded parameter set for two commits, and its own
  footer said no parameter was sourced while twelve were. Reports now carry the SHA-256 of the
  parameters they describe, and a test fails when the committed report drifts.
- A citation claimed a growth rate its own figures contradicted (27% and 2.01% per month, from
  volumes that give 26.73% and 1.99%). Corrected, and the loader now refuses citations that name no
  table, page or section.
